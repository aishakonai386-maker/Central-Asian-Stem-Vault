RESEARCH

Research

Research is not only for university students or scientists in labs. You can start now — even from a small town, even without expensive equipment.

What is research, really?

Research is a structured way of answering a question you genuinely care about. It is not about memorizing facts. It is about asking "why" and "how" — and then finding evidence to support your answer. Research teaches you to think independently, gather reliable information, and draw conclusions from your own observations.

Why should you do research?

01

It builds real skills

Research teaches you critical thinking, problem-solving, and communication skills that matter in university and any career.

02

It opens doors

A research project can become a competition entry, a university application highlight, or even a published paper.

03

It solves local problems

You understand your community better than anyone. Research lets you tackle real issues that matter where you live.

04

It is free to start

You don't need a lab. You need curiosity, a notebook, and access to the internet or a library.

How to do research: step by step

Every research project follows the same basic path. Here is the process in simple steps.

STEP 1

Choose a topic you care about

Pick something that genuinely interests you — a local environmental issue, a problem in your school, or a question from your favorite class. It should be specific enough to study.

STEP 2

Turn it into a question

A good research question is clear and testable. Instead of "Is pollution bad?", try: "How does water quality differ between upstream and downstream of our village?"

STEP 3

Read what others found

Before you start, look up existing research on your topic. This is called a literature review. It tells you what is already known and what gap you can fill.

STEP 4

Make a plan

Write down your hypothesis (your educated guess), your method, and what you will measure. Keep it simple and realistic with the resources you have.

STEP 5

Collect your data

This can be surveys, observations, experiments, or interviews. Even a notebook and a phone camera can be research tools.

STEP 6

Write it up and share

A simple structure: Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion, Conclusion. Then present your findings to your class, community, or a competition.

How to write a research paper

A research paper follows a standard structure. Each section has a specific job.

SECTION

Introduction

What is your question, and why does it matter? Describe the problem and state your hypothesis.

SECTION

Methods

How did you do it? Describe your procedure so clearly that someone else could repeat it exactly.

SECTION

Results

What did you find? Present your data — numbers, tables, or graphs. Do not interpret yet. Just report.

SECTION

Discussion

What do your results mean? Connect them to your hypothesis and to other research. Be honest about limitations.

You don't need a lab to do research.

Some of the best research starts with a simple question about your own community. Your local knowledge is a real advantage. Start small, stay curious, and ask for help when you need it — teachers, mentors, and online communities are there for you.

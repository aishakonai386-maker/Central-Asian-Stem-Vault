ACADEMIC STRATEGY

A letter from someone who's been there

No lectures. No "top 10 hacks". Just honest advice from someone a few steps ahead of you, who also grew up without private tutors and expensive programs.

Hey. I know what it feels like to sit in a small classroom and wonder if you're falling behind students in the capital. Or to see people online with tutors, laptops, coding clubs — and think "they have everything I don't."

Here's the truth: the gap between you and them is smaller than it looks. Most of what they have access to is free and available to you too, if you know where to look. What actually separates students isn't resources — it's strategy and consistency.

This page is what I wish someone had told me at 14. Read it slowly. Come back to it when you feel stuck. And most importantly — do a little every day. Not a lot. A little.

Six things that actually matter

Forget the "study hacks". These are the habits that quietly separate successful students from everyone else — and none of them require money.

01

Protect your focus

Your phone is your biggest enemy, not your school. Study in 25-minute blocks with your phone in another room. That one habit will double what you learn in a week.

02

Read for 20 minutes a day

In English — anything. News, fiction, Wikipedia, subtitles on shows. Reading is the single fastest way to improve in English, and English unlocks 90% of the world's free resources.

03

Go deep on ONE subject

Don't try to be good at everything. Pick one subject — math, coding, biology, whatever pulls you — and go deeper than your class. That depth becomes your superpower in applications.

04

Start a small project

A website. A small research on your village's water. A coding game. Anything you built yourself. Projects teach you more than any course, and they're what universities remember.

05

Find one person ahead of you

A teacher, an older student, someone on a forum or a Discord. Ask them questions. You don't need ten mentors — just one person who's a few steps ahead and willing to help.

06

Stop waiting to feel ready

You'll never feel ready. Apply to the program anyway. Enter the competition anyway. Send the email anyway. Most of the "prepared" students just started earlier — that's it.

If I were you, here's my plan

Not a schedule. Just what I'd focus on, if I had four years of high school ahead of me and no special resources.

YEAR 1–2

Build your base

English, math, and one science you love. Focus on fundamentals — they make everything later easier. Do one small project. Join one olympiad, even if you don't win.

YEAR 3

Go deeper

Pick your main subject and go beyond school. Take free online courses. Start your own research or project. Enter national competitions. You're building your story now.

YEAR 4

Apply and finish strong

Scholarships, universities, programs. Your earlier work becomes your application. Don't panic-apply — you'll already have a real story if you built it over the years.

ALWAYS

Keep learning outside school

School gives you the basics. Your edge comes from what you do in the evenings, on weekends, and during holidays. That's where the real growth happens.

Free tools you should be using

These are the sites and platforms I'd use if I were starting again today. All free, all accessible from a phone with internet.

Khan Academy

Free courses in math, science, English, and more. Khan Academy is basically a free school from kindergarten to university. Start here first.

Coursera & edX

University courses from top schools — free to audit. You won't get a certificate unless you pay, but the knowledge is completely free.

Codeforces & LeetCode

If you're into coding, these are where real programmers practice. Start with easy problems and solve one every day.

AoPS (Art of Problem Solving)

The gold standard for math olympiad prep. Has a free forum with thousands of solved problems and a community happy to help you.

MIT OpenCourseWare

Real MIT lectures — free, forever. Yes, it's hard. You don't need to understand everything — just watching one video a week changes how you think.

YouTube

Underrated. 3Blue1Brown for math, CrashCourse for everything else, freeCodeCamp for coding. Free education from the best teachers in the world.

You are not behind. You are early.

The students who succeed from small towns aren't smarter. They just started before it felt comfortable, kept going when it felt pointless, and stopped comparing themselves to people with more advantages. Your starting point is not your ending point. Start today — even if it's just 20 minutes. That's enough.

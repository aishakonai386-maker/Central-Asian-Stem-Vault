COMPETITIONS

Competitions

Competitions are one of the best ways to grow — they test your knowledge, connect you with other students, and open doors. Many are completely free and open to students from any town or city.

Why should you enter competitions?

You don't need to win to benefit. Entering competitions helps you learn faster, meet motivated students, and build a strong application for universities and scholarships.

Push yourself

Competitions challenge you to go deeper than classroom material — real problems, real thinking.

Meet peers

You'll connect with students from across your country and the world who share your interests.

Strengthen applications

Universities and scholarship programs value competition experience — even if you don't win.

What kinds of competitions exist?

Competitions come in many forms — pick the ones that match your interests and skills.

Subject Olympiads

Math, physics, chemistry, biology, informatics. National and international levels.

Robotics & Engineering

Build robots, drones, or machines. Often team-based and hands-on.

Hackathons & Coding

Solve problems with code in 24–48 hours. Great for aspiring programmers.

Research Competitions

Present original research on science, math, or engineering topics.

Essay & Innovation

Write about science, propose solutions, or pitch ideas for real-world problems.

Team Challenges

Case competitions, quizzes, and problem-solving events with teams.

Competitions to know about

These competitions are open to high school students and many are free to enter. Some are international — you can often participate online or through your school.

International Math Olympiad (IMO)

The most prestigious math competition in the world. Students compete in national teams. You start by doing well in national olympiads.

How to prepare

You do not need a private tutor or expensive courses. Consistent practice and the right free resources are more than enough.

Start early

Give yourself months, not weeks. Even 30 minutes of focused practice a day adds up fast.

Use free resources

Khan Academy, AoPS (Art of Problem Solving), Codeforces, and past olympiad papers are free online.

Find a mentor or team

A teacher, older student, or online community can help you prepare faster and stay motivated.

You don't have to be the best to compete.

Many top competitors started in small towns with no special resources — just curiosity and consistent effort. The first competition is the hardest. After that, every attempt makes you stronger. Start with a local or national olympiad, and build from there.
